## Item (2) — v2 spec written after check 19: what b3 now is; generic vs probe-shaped — **CONCERN (structural), adequately disclosed in README, but the claim wording overreaches**

**What b3 is, precisely.** b3 is simultaneously (a) the **selection set** for every comparative number in checks 19–22 (finder/full/evicted/control included) and (b) the **spec-development set**: the v1→v2 amendment, the scorer change (context → no-context), and the diagnosis targets (the 38 probe constraint sentences via ft_diag.py) were all derived from b3 misses. Every b3 number is therefore a development-set fit. It can rank design choices; it is evidence of nothing about transfer. The README says exactly this (“development result on the selection set, not transfer”) — but the claim sentence in the brief (“pins 33/56 … reaches the full-context ceiling”) drops the qualifier and must never be quoted without it.

**Probe-shaped case (strong).** Check 19 enumerates the missed shapes: “finish with this exact postscript line”, “begin with the exact title <<...>>”, “exactly 5 bullet points”, “in lowercase letters only”. LABELS.md v2’s examples for the new scope are “begin with the title <<...>>” and “end with a P.S.” — the spec’s canonical illustrations are the probe’s failures, down to the `<<...>>` template. The kimi-scope batch teaches precisely those shapes: “Write the whole summary in the second person.” (whole-artifact formatting, no scope words, `hard: true` — structurally identical to “in lowercase letters only”), “Every flashcard must end with a one-line mnemonic.” (end-marker ≈ postscript), “No bullet points in the study guide we’re building” (≈ bullet constraint). Note also the README’s own admission: the taxonomy finder “had learned” task scope from the same benchmark family — v2 moves the classifier’s objective toward the finder’s prior, i.e., toward the metric. A spec written *because* a metric punished its absence is shaped by that metric, however generally it is phrased.

**Generic case (also strong, and I think correct at the concept level).** The conversation / task-artifact / single-reply trichotomy is a natural linguistic partition any retention designer needs; it names no keywords, counts, or benchmark categories beyond illustrative examples. The scope batch covers all three scopes, including anti-probe cases (“For this one answer only, reply in bullet form.” → none, hard). Opus’s review applied the notion with a stated decision procedure that cuts across benchmark shapes: whole-deliverable tone kept, sentence-level edits rejected; maintainable numeric targets kept, bare operations rejected; request-headed constraint sentences rejected (a shape *absent* from the b3 diagnosis, rejected anyway — 366/461 rejections is not a rubber stamp). The false-positive analysis (pinned one-off exceptions misgovern later turns) is benchmark-independent reasoning.

**Synthesis.** Both are true and they operate at different levels: the *concept* is generic; the *timing, examples, and proposal distribution* are probe-shaped. “Generic classifier” is defensible as a description of the artifact; it is not defensible as a description of the process by which that artifact’s spec reached v2. The claim should read “generic in coverage, developed against the b3 selection set.”
