**Task-level expert-routing bias on Qwen3 MoE — research and proposed oracle screen**
2026-09-05; live primary-source search, CPU/source inspection only. No model/GPU launch, process signals, sealed IFEval/BFCL reads, or edits outside this memo. Archived protocol/state read; this task’s explicit one-file restriction supersedes ledger-writing rules.

**Recommendation: test one frozen 30B-A3B router-bias oracle; estimated full-screen PASS probability 20%.**
Brian’s proposal has direct precedents, including experiments on the intended trunk. A slow task register can bias the fast token router without changing expert weights. Published evidence supports behavioral influence; it does not establish reliable task induction plus retained-history SET–HOLD–SWITCH–CLEAR.
Stencil’s [Miller assessment](astra-drift-assessment.md), [checks 30–39](quick-checks/README.md), [head-gating hypothesis #4](hypotheses-fable.md), and [FOCUS-3 design](focus3-design-astra.md) govern the interpretation: frozen competencies, operand-sensitive answers, transient selection, suppression and behavioral release. The residual-vector/packet/coordinate failures do not test MoE routing; placement and own-output masking remain the supported engineering direction.

Evidence from 2024–2026 follows. Changes are percentage points unless explicitly labelled relative; these are authors’ measurements, not replications here.

For MoTE/SteerMoE/RICE/Ban&Pick, response-level results support effects while intervention remains enabled; they do not demonstrate unrefreshed multi-turn HOLD, within-history task switching, or release after removal. I found no complete retained-history SET–HOLD–SWITCH–CLEAR test in the inspected papers. Unchanged weights and disabling a hook do not erase steered answers or downstream KV.
Distinguish task bias from [loss-free balancing (2024)](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.15664): the latter updates selection biases from historical load during training and leaves mixture weights unbiased. Nor is this [Expert Choice](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.09368), the 2022 architecture where experts select token buckets. Neither establishes classifier-driven task control; Qwen3 uses token-choice top-k.

Qwen3 implementation and machine feasibility:

| Original checkpoint | Layers; hidden width; expert FFN width | Routing; attention heads Q/KV |
|---|---|---|
| [Qwen3-30B-A3B config](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-30B-A3B/blob/main/config.json) | 48; 2,048; 768 | 128 experts/layer, top-8, zero shared; 32/4, head dimension 128 |
| [Qwen3-235B-A22B config](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-235B-A22B/blob/main/config.json) | 94; 4,096; 1,536 | 128 experts/layer, top-8, zero shared; 64/4, head dimension 128 |

All layers are MoE (`decoder_sparse_step=1; mlp_only_layers=[]`); expert identities are layer-local: 6,144 versus 12,032 distinct pairs. Qwen explicitly removed shared experts and trained with global-batch auxiliary balancing to permit specialization; this does not force each request to use experts uniformly. [Technical report, §2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09388)
Recomputed from the live safetensor-index metadata: [30B weights](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-30B-A3B/blob/main/model.safetensors.index.json) = 61,064,245,248 bytes = 56.87 GiB bf16; [235B weights](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-235B-A22B/blob/main/model.safetensors.index.json) = 470,187,269,120 bytes = 437.90 GiB. All experts must reside somewhere; “3B active” does not mean a 6 GB weight footprint.
30B fits a free 128 GB machine with short contexts; budget roughly 65–80 GiB working memory including framework/workspace headroom, an estimate requiring measurement. 235B bf16 cannot fit. GB10 uses 128 GB shared CPU/GPU memory, not separate VRAM; keep OS/classifier headroom. [NVIDIA hardware specification](https://docs.nvidia.com/dgx/dgx-spark/hardware.html)
Derived bf16 KV per sequence: `2 × layers × 4 KV heads × 128 × 2 bytes` = 96 KiB/token on 30B, 188 KiB on 235B; at 8,192 tokens: 0.75/1.469 GiB. Cache cloning/batching multiplies this; use short episodes and avoid retaining every full logits tensor.
Gate location: `model.layers[l].mlp.gate`, inside `Qwen3MoeSparseMoeBlock`, after attention residual addition and `post_attention_layernorm`. The bias-free linear router maps hidden states to 128 scores; float32 softmax → top-8 → sum-to-one renormalization → expert mixture → residual addition. `experts.*.gate_proj` is SwiGLU, not the router. [Original Transformers implementation](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/huggingface/transformers/v4.51.3/src/transformers/models/qwen3_moe/modeling_qwen3_moe.py)
Version trap verified by reading installed Transformers 5.16.1 source: `gate=Qwen3MoeTopKRouter` returns `(logits, weights, indices)`; the block discards logits. A forward hook altering tuple item zero alone is a NO-OP. Override the router before its softmax, or recompute and return all three outputs. Every layer is accessible. [5.16.1 source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/huggingface/transformers/v5.16.1/src/transformers/models/qwen3_moe/modeling_qwen3_moe.py)
`output_router_logits=True` is observation, not actuation. Config auxiliary coefficient is 0.001; inference has no adaptive load correction. Profile with streaming counters; disable the recorder afterward. Prove changed expert indices/weights on nonzero tests and exact original-path behavior when OFF; preserve the original dtype/casting order.
Throughput is kernel-dependent, not dense-3B speed by definition: Qwen’s H20/Transformers-4.51.3 bf16 result is 1.89 tokens/s, versus SGLang 137.18, at one input token and 2,048 output tokens. Their metric counts input+output tokens, so long-input rows are not decode rates. Neither transfers directly to GB10 plus custom masks/hooks. [Official speed benchmark](https://qwen.readthedocs.io/en/latest/getting_started/speed_benchmark.html)
With GB10’s 273 GB/s bandwidth, a crude 6–7 GB active-weight read/token suggests an idealized ~40 tokens/s ceiling before attention/dispatch costs; actual custom eager execution can be far slower. Plan around a measured pilot, with an automatic cost stop; do not promise four-hour completion from active parameter count.

Does task type identify a usable profile?

- Positive measurements: ESFT’s layer-averaged top-six overlap is near six within tasks and near zero between tasks on its DeepSeek backbone (§3.2). This is not a Qwen3 measurement. [ESFT](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01906)
- Qwen30B has measured math/medicine frequency contrasts and language/task dissociation: at activation-frequency difference ≥0.3, the multilingual study finds zero overlap between task-specialized and multilingual-specialized sets across four models. Domain differences are weaker than language differences; this does not show math/code/JSON form disjoint skill modules. [Multilingual Routing, §§4–5](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.04694)
- Counterweight: Mixtral routing across ArXiv/PubMed/philosophy is similar, with stronger syntax effects; a 2026 causal Command A+ study finds only one of six hypothesized families robustly selective across its strict tests, while its Qwen30B positive control recovers language/task separation. Routing mass alone fails to establish selective causal importance. [Mixtral §5](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.04088), [causal modularity study](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.25092)
- I found no validated Qwen3 four-way code/prose/math/structured-output routing classifier with accuracy and confusion matrix. JSON may mostly recruit punctuation/token specialists shared with code. Ascending and descending sorting are especially likely to share circuits; a routable broad domain is not necessarily an addressable opposite rule.

Proposed QUICK ORACLE SCREEN — prospective, no execution authorized by this memo:

1. Freeze exact checkpoint revision (original `Qwen/Qwen3-30B-A3B`, not Base/2507), tokenizer/template, bf16, greedy non-thinking mode, source hashes and checkers before any run. Trunk/router weights never train; only empirical profiles and setup dose selection occur.
2. Lineage: fresh authored generators only. Seeds 9056501 profile, 9056502 competence, 9056503 dose/timing setup, 9056504 final, 9056505 permutations/arm order. Disjoint payload hashes AND expression/template families across profile/setup/final; no benchmark prompts, answers, extracted paper expert IDs, or FOCUS-2 evaluation records. Freeze exclusions/manifests using permitted synthetic sources only.
3. Consider these three pairs in fixed priority order; select the first passing competence and profile eligibility, then run exactly one dose grid. A failed actuator grid ends this screen; no substitution of another pair afterward.

| Pair | Same visible novel input; A versus B | Executable checker; expected distinction |
|---|---|---|
| P1, preferred | Small arithmetic expression tree: Python `solve()` source versus controlled English description of its operations. | Parse Python AST and require the input operators/leaves, then evaluate with a restricted arithmetic interpreter; parse English through a frozen finite grammar back into the same tree. Code/prose is plausibly more distinct than opposing sorts; grammar must admit natural cued wording. |
| P2 | Same expression tree: Python compilation versus numerical evaluation. | Same AST checker versus exact integer result from the tree; exclude accidental output collisions. Code/math contrast, but shared arithmetic experts are likely. |
| P3 | Tiny table/filter specification: SQL query versus JSON of selected rows. | Parse restricted SELECT/WHERE SQL and run read-only against three fresh tiny databases; compare to reference relational execution. JSON schema plus exact rows checks the other skill. Code/structured-output contrast; punctuation sharing may defeat separation. |

4. Common carrier: one stable system schema `{"tag":<session tag>,"answer":<string>}`, plus a default to echo the compact payload string when no task is active. Keep operands visible. Preserve the tag/schema independently of the task cue, following FOCUS-2d’s split-cue lesson. Candidate answers fit 64 new tokens; no grammar-constrained decoding that would supply the selected skill.
5. Competence: 16 fresh operands per candidate; A-cued and B-cued each ≥14/16 checker-correct, OFF-default ≥15/16; zero structural breaks. Put cues inside the CURRENT user request, before the ordinary assistant header. Failure means INELIGIBLE, not steering failure. Use all outputs, not only successful exemplars.
6. Profile extraction: 32 separate paired operands/candidate with cued, checker-defined canonical completions, teacher-forced solely for measurement. Primary `f[s,l,e]` is per-example mean top-8 selection frequency over the final eight IDENTICAL prompt-suffix tokens, averaged over examples. Exclude the differing cue tokens; average each operand equally.
7. Also record answer-token selection frequencies, centered mean logits, router mass, and eighth-minus-ninth logit margins by layer. These diagnose output-syntax confounding and dose sensitivity; they do not become outcome-selected alternative profile recipes. Shared-suffix primary profiling deliberately asks for a task signal before different answer glyphs are emitted.
8. Compute `sigma[l]` from pooled within-token router-logit SD on profile suffixes; `P[s,l,e]=sigma[l]*f[s,l,e]`, `Pmean=(P[A]+P[B])/2`. Apply `z'[l,t,e]=z[l,t,e]+alpha*(P[s,l,e]-Pmean[l,e])`; OFF branches straight to original routing. No division by a tiny A−B norm, no forcing experts, no changed k, no weight training.
9. Same-experts eligibility: in at least one proposed layer band, frequency-profile TV `mean_l(sum_e(abs(fA-fB))/16)` ≥0.10 and split-half contrast cosine ≥0.5. Require paired-label permutation p≤.05 with a maximum-statistic correction across all three pairs × three bands (9,999 swaps). If none qualifies: INELIGIBLE/SAME-EXPERTS under this statistic, not proof of mathematical identity or absence of every possible controller.
10. Freeze nine nonzero cells: alpha {0.5,1,2} × layers {7–34,32–47,0–47}, all zero-indexed. On eight setup operands/cell, test both addresses and a norm-preserving shuffled profile; share OFF/text controls. Select highest worst-direction strict success, then fewer breaks, then smaller alpha/fewer layers; require each direction ≥6/8 and at most one broken reply. No final-based adjustment.
11. Breakage calibration: 32 separately authored short neutral completions, scored teacher-forced under each bias and OFF. Each direction’s mean NLL increase must be ≤0.10 nat/token (perplexity ratio ≤exp(.10)=1.1052). This is a local collateral gate, not a general perplexity guarantee. Log top-8 changes and selected mass; large route change alone earns no success.
12. Apply the task bias during new-request prefill AND every decoding position through EOS, so it can affect the first generated token. The register changes only at scripted SET/SWITCH/BACK/CLEAR boundaries, remaining fixed through HOLD and within each answer. Do not recompute historical KV under a new bias.
13. Final arms: C correct A/B/OFF; S swapped A↔B, same OFF; H fixed per-layer expert-index permutations of profiles, preserving values/norm and A/B opposition; O OFF throughout; T textual task updates inside the current requests at SET/SWITCH/BACK, router OFF. Shuffling same-task donor examples would not be a meaningful null.
14. Run 32 fresh complete episodes, balanced across initial A/B and 0/256-token irrelevant delay in the HOLD request. Six scored replies: SET→HOLD→SWITCH→BACK→CLEAR1→CLEAR2. Keep valid chat pairs and every arm’s actual unscreened answers/EOS; no answer replacement, physical cache edits, cue refresh at HOLD, or output masking in this router-only screen.
15. CLEAR sets the router address to OFF; both subsequent requests use the same generic payload request/default, without a new “copy” instruction that could overwhelm residue. Check default echo, absence of both old task modes, and the persistent tag at both clears. T uses explicit release once at CLEAR1, then no refresh at CLEAR2; this is a stronger text-based control, disclosed.
16. Four non-text arms start from identical prompt/KV hashes. They diverge after generating; compare them by episode, not by claiming later KV equality. Additionally fork each C pre-SWITCH cache for two one-reply replays, A and B, with identical new request and operands: at least 24/32 paired replays must follow both corresponding donor tasks.
17. Define structural breakage separately: unparseable/non-object JSON, absent/empty/non-string answer, truncation without natural terminal, or a four-token block repeated ≥8 times. Valid code that computes the wrong answer is a skill failure; missing/wrong tag is collateral failure. Invalid or ambiguous responses count as failures, never successful clearance.
18. Final eligibility: T completes the entire intended sequence in ≥28/32 episodes, O repeats the default through all six replies in ≥30/32. Otherwise INELIGIBLE/task-or-history harness. C must have ≥24/32 complete six-step successes and ≥28/32 at EACH step; S must follow its swapped donor sequence in ≥24/32. C and S each need ≥12/16 complete successes in EACH initial-direction stratum, scoring S against its donors.
19. Additional PASS gates: C has ≥8 more intended-sequence successes than EACH of S/H/O; C and S introduce ZERO broken episodes or tag failures relative to O. H must remain structurally valid in ≥30/32, otherwise its null is damaged and the screen is INCONCLUSIVE. C must clear BOTH requests in ≥30/32 with ≤1 episode imposing either old mode.
20. Existence test: C versus each S/H/O on complete intended-sequence success, one-sided exact McNemar with Holm correction over three tests, familywise .05. For b=C-only successes and c=control-only successes, p=Pr[Binom(b+c,.5)≥b], p=1 if b+c=0. Require all three; practical margins alone cannot establish evidence. Report per-direction/delay counts and paired discordances.
21. Mechanical checks precede behavioral interpretation: OFF reproduces original router scores/indices and next-token logits on identical input/KV; nonzero bias demonstrably reaches expert dispatch; state/cue/operand hashes establish no answer leakage. Invalid instrumentation is INVALID; missing jobs/cost exhaustion INCOMPLETE; valid eligible runs missing a gate FAIL. Freeze this order before running.
22. Cost ceiling ≤4 GPU-h inclusive: load, competence/profile ≤0.6 h; setup/collateral/throughput ≤0.6 h; final/replays ≤2.3 h; reserve 0.5 h. These are planning allocations, not timings. After dose selection, time one full five-arm setup episode at the 256-token delay, including clear/replays; use the slowest stage costs. Final alone is 32×5×6×64=61,440 capped decode tokens; replays add 4,096. Profile passes are prefill work. Project every remaining job from measured slowest relevant pilot cost with 25% reserve; stop before launching an over-cap matrix.
23. At 1.89 tokens/s the capped final alone would require ~9.03 h, so slow stock execution cannot justify a launch. Use one already-available compatible expert kernel if verified with the custom router/mask path; no kernel-development project or engine migration inside this screen. Check a cooperative deadline between steps; return naturally, never signal processes. A timing refusal is an informative INCOMPLETE/COST result.
24. Same-run raw records: every prompt/output/token/EOS, seed/payload family, task epoch, cue placement, KV/hash/position provenance, applied per-layer bias, routing counts/margins/selected mass, checkers, neutral NLL, breakage/tag flags, peak memory and charged wall time. Smoke-assert the writer’s field contract; no aggregate-only seal or regenerated records.
25. Claim ceiling: an oracle-selected, task-constant routing profile controls these frozen, pre-demonstrated competencies on this synthetic family and releases under tested histories. P1/P3 may establish semantic serialization/control of output mode, not separate reasoning algorithms. Persistent bias demonstrates externally maintained HOLD, not self-sustaining memory, autonomous classification, oscillator advantage, benchmark transfer or Miller’s biological theory.

Composition with FOCUS-3 and migration from the dense trunk:

- Keep register admission/relations, authority/scope, current-request placement, and own-output provenance masks. Add a SEPARATE calibrated task-type classifier over the authorized current request plus active task register; its outputs select A/B/OFF profiles. Admission salience and pairwise supersedes/cancels/etc. are not task classification. Ambiguous/mixed work → OFF; no per-token classifier chatter or fresh generated instructions.
- The register carries the slow state; router scores remain input-dependent at every token. Keep exact arbitrary constraints/facts as placed text. A “code” address cannot encode a user’s particular schema, tag, title or cancellation scope. First test the classifier against the frozen oracle policy on new disjoint authored data; preserve the inherited admission model’s lineage disclosure.
- Use a MoE-specific `Focus3Config(Qwen3MoeConfig)`/`Focus3ForCausalLM(Qwen3MoeForCausalLM)`, with router modification inside custom model code. Ship unchanged trunk shards, both tokenizer sets, classifier bundle, routing_profiles.safetensors, config/auto_map, provenance and pinned dependencies in ONE HF repo. A 48×128 float32 profile is only 24 KiB/task. Preserve unchanged trunk state-dict keys; keep profiles as separately loaded immutable buffers.
- Task/bias epochs live in `Focus3Cache` per session, not mutable model-global hooks. Consume a pending sampled token under its sampling epoch before changing the register/bias. Clear removes bias immediately; subsequent own-output masking may limit behavioral residue, but old user cues and other downstream KVs remain. If router-only CLEAR fails, the combined package is a separate claim, not a rescue labelled oracle PASS.
- Keep the [FOCUS-3](focus3-design-astra.md) explicit physical positions, eager/SDPA mask path, history/provenance accounting, and cache-sharing restrictions. Prefix reuse requires matching register/mask/bias epochs, not only token IDs. The original dense Stencil block loader is not a drop-in MoE loader; new expert storage/dispatch must preserve pretrained values. One HF repo still requires custom-code support; stock vLLM/SGLang needs an adapter.
- Trunk change: 4B’s 36 layers/8 KV heads become 48 layers/4 KV heads; weight memory rises roughly eightfold although active compute is comparable in scale. Freshly verify tokenizer/template/thinking behavior, schema/tag adherence, copy/sort competence, role precedence, current-request placement, delayed HOLD, two-request CLEAR, masking and task-resume/unmask effects. None inherits a 4B certificate. [Qwen architecture](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09388)
- A later, separately budgeted FOCUS-2 migration spot-check should use 16 new sort episodes, both directions and 0/256 delays, with ordinary history, text placement, and text+own-output-mask arms. Preserve permanent schema/tag carriers as in [FOCUS-2d registration](../LEDGER-PLAN.md); measure collateral fields separately from malformed/truncated output. Do not reuse/tune on FOCUS-2d inputs or infer its pending outcome.

**Odds and cuts:** 20% subjective probability of the full oracle screen passing, including eligibility, safe induction, hold, switch and clear within cost; roughly 50% that some safe single-shot addressed behavior moves. These are judgment calls, not empirical posterior estimates.
The single biggest risk is that the profile describes tokens produced AFTER a task is selected, while task choice lives in shared attention/residual computation: bias then changes syntax or damages processing without selecting the intended operation. Shared-suffix profiling and operand-sensitive counterfactual checks expose this.
Cut 235B, fine-tuning/LoRA/router training, expert-count changes, imported benchmark profiles, asc/desc as the lead pair, full expert atlases, per-token controllers, oscillators, actuator rescue variants and serving-engine work. Spend once on eligibility→one grid→one retained-history screen. Keep classifier fitting and the combined register/mask product conditional on that evidence.
