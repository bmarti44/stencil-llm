This matters: a whole-output hash monitor alone misses this witness. It also follows that the user’s broader “thousands” description should not be treated as thousands of independent labelled examples; this audit establishes1,664 main rows, not an inventory of every historical diagnostic call.

A **retrospective baseline**, not a prospective test: alarm before round t whenever round t−1 was a nonwrite. Across the1,080 eligible next-round R/N/T opportunities, it makes99 alarms:96 are followed by a nonwrite and three by a write. That is96/99 precision and96/101 recall for next-round nonwrites, dominated almost entirely by obsolete pilot5 failures. It anticipates **zero of the11 nonwrite streak onsets**, including six round0 onsets. A high pooled score can therefore mean “recognizes yesterday’s error,” with no ability to predict the first error or transfer to the repaired harness.

The records carry text, token IDs, execution metadata, and hidden checker outcomes. They do not carry activation vectors. A file inventory of these three pilot directories found **zero `.npy` or `.npz` files**. All1,152 R/N/T main HTTP choices have both `logprobs` and `prompt_logprobs` null. I did not exhaustively rule out arbitrarily named tensors elsewhere; I did establish that these saved records do not provide hidden-state or entropy training features. Token IDs and hashes cannot recover them on CPU without a trunk forward pass. Pilot1’s historical hidden-state captures do not cure its check45 eligibility failure.

Labels also need a new explicit contract. `src/stencil/focus/slab2.py:607–715` distinguishes applicability, writes, changed-code indentation, retired-trait presence, integration, wrong task/path and breakage. `success=None` under a pending floor is **not failure**. An early parser exit is **not evidence of zero rule violations**. Pilot5/6 retain the subsequently removed delivery_scope field. Proposed detectors must neither learn that obsolete field nor call a verbose round’s inapplicable compact-format flag a violation.

Use separate outcomes: **transport/parse failure**, **applicable live-obligation violation**, **wrong target**, **semantic failure**, and **persistent failure of the same obligation across an unchanged applicability window**. Define a lock operationally as at least two consecutive applicable failures, not an assertion of irreversible absorption. A revoked obligation is relapse only with evidence of prior enactment, current retirement and current unauthorized enactment; merely mentioning it is insufficient. Trainable features may use public tool receipts and authenticated effective state, but hidden integration results and saved checker labels are scoring-only, including at earlier turns. Otherwise “cheap detection” becomes oracle leakage.

**Rank1 — public contract checker plus failure-persistence state machine**

What: after generation but **before accepting an action**, parse the declared response protocol, validate target and syntax, check applicable typed obligations and available public tests. Emit a structured reason and obligation ID. Record repeat failure of the same obligation across still-applicable rounds; mark risk for the next turn. This catches the first observable failure without pretending to predict it before generation. Text/AST parsing lives in the output verifier; the register receives no regex-derived add/supersede/complete operation.

Why first: pilot5’s visible fence failures and pilot7’s explicit compact-trailer violations do not need a learned detector. OpenHands supplies the monitor pattern; our existing parser, public executor metadata and typed register supply much of the implementation. The result can travel as controller code in one HF bundle, with unchanged trunk and no second model. Retirement remains a versioned mask operation using authenticated IDs; alarms never delete rules or history. **Effort:3–5 engineer-hours** for a read-only shadow monitor, label audit and replay report; production transactional integration is additional work.

**Rank2 — CPU classifier of next-turn risk from public trajectory features**

What: fixed L2 logistic regression on a small feature vector: previous public failure categories, consecutive-failure count, public pass/fail counts, AST/artifact change magnitude, changed-target indicator, public active-obligation change indicator, number/age of live and retired obligations, prompt/output length ratios and output-to-previous-output similarity. Use typed state, never episode ID, arm name, pilot number, task names, hidden labels or future information. This should predict failure recurrence and possible new failure at a rule change. Preserve an “unknown” feature when public tests are unavailable. A fixed hashed-token cosine comparator needs no fitted vocabulary or encoder download; it is a baseline only, outside registration.

Why second: the new CPU census shows a nearly free recurrence signal, and the exact-text-copy baseline misses it. A fitted model might distinguish temporary syntax errors from persistent failures or identify rule-change risk. That incremental gain is unmeasured. No trunk tuning, no extra HF asset beyond a tiny coefficient/scaler sidecar in the same bundle, and no register mutations are needed. **Effort:5–8 engineer-hours**, mainly lineage/feature timing and grouped evaluation rather than model fitting. Main risks: eight groups, pseudo-replication, learning protocol defects, high apparent AUC from already-failing lanes, and losing calibration when repair changes the histories.

Quick test, **0 GPU-hours**: use next-round opportunities from the same1,152 records, at most1,080 prediction rows. Outer leave-one-base-episode-out CV; fit/scaling with seed0 and fixed `C=1`, no feature search. Inner episode folds choose one threshold maximizing recall subject to≤20% trigger rate and precision≥.80; if no threshold qualifies, abstain and fail the recall bar. Equal total fit weight per episode, with per-pilot score tables. Labels for t are nonwrite OR applicable live-obligation violation OR wrong target OR hidden semantic failure, scored separately as well as combined; hidden outcomes enter the target only. Compare with prior-public-failure carry-forward, majority/base-rate, length-only, and fixed current-rule/output similarity. Current output similarity is legal only when predicting t+1.

Prewritten reading: GO only if outer-fold AUROC≥.80, paired episode-bootstrap95% lower bound on AUROC gain over the strongest baseline>0, and point gain≥.05; at the frozen threshold precision≥.80, recall≥.50, triggers≤20%, Brier better than the training-prior baseline.
