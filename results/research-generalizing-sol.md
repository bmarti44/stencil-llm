# Generalizing selection: recommendation and minimum decisive experiment

*Research date: 2026-09-02. `[V]` = verified in the linked primary paper/code or Stencil artifact; `[I]` = my inference/synthesis (not a reported result).*

## Decision — ranked

1. **Build a reversible two-tier memory, not a better irreversible guess.** Keep protocol-level invariants (system/tool schemas), a recent KV window, and selected KVs; archive *every* evicted span losslessly in CPU RAM/disk. At each new turn, retrieve from the archive with the now-known query, echo the raw spans, and optionally echo a rolling structured state note. This is the smallest change that removes G1's irreducibly uncertain requirement—predicting an unknown future query—while preserving Stencil's verified retention mechanism. `[V]` Locally, `pinned_echo` is 48/56 versus full-context 44 and random-column control 18; BFCL provenance is 42% current user, 20% earlier user, 17% earlier tool output, and 37% derived, while `salience2` recall is only .065 ([H1′ reviews](./h1p-review-sol.md), [BFCL audit](./agentic-salience-review-fable.md)). `[I]` Role-based protection is a protocol rule, not a benchmark regex; the archive makes selection errors recoverable.
2. **Run G0/G1 as a bounded scientific bet and as an admission/ranking feature—not as the sole memory.** Learned future-utility retention is credible prior art, but cross-domain transfer is unproved for actual tool calls. G1 succeeds only if it wins on a completely unseen corpus *and* improves end-task behavior under joint eviction.
3. **Keep H2O/SnapKV/TOVA/StreamingLLM/self-summary as controls. Do not ship them alone.** Attention is evidence of past use, not future need; summaries are lossy and add model calls.

## What the literature actually establishes

**No KV selector I could verify has evidence on both constraint-style instruction following and real multi-turn tool/agent success (BFCL/τ-bench/WebArena).** `[V]` The closest accepted learned method, [TRIM-KV (ICLR 2026)](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/c3887741ee4e655494770be95303383f-Abstract-Conference.html), learns per-token/per-head creation-time gates by frozen-model distillation and covers math, LongProc, LongMemEval, LongBenchV2, and SCBench—not tool calling. On Qwen3-4B LongMemEvalS, full cache scored 49.4; at 32K/16K/8K KVs, StreamingLLM scored 27.6/19.0/13.0, SnapKV 27.8/18.2/15.8, and TRIM-KV 44.8/38.6/34.0. Yet on SCBench random-KV retrieval at budget 4096, full scored 66 and **every compressed method, including TRIM-KV, scored 0**. `[V]` [KVP](https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/evict) learns 112 per-head policies from *future attention mass* (not downstream loss), transfers from ~4K training to RULER-128K and zero-shot BoolQ/GovReport/LongBench, and tests OASST2 perplexity—not instruction adherence or agent success. Its 4,649 OASST traces took <2 hours on 56 GPUs and ~1.2 TB; policy training took <30 minutes on 8 H100s.

`[V]` [SCBench (ICLR 2025)](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/a540b17fb2295c736d5afd6c507acf66-Paper-Conference.pdf) is the decisive heavy-hitter failure: 931 sessions/4,853 queries show that important KVs change between turns. On Llama-3.1-8B, full-cache multi-turn average was 48.7 versus MInference 42.5, KIVI 32.0, PyramidKV 21.0, SnapKV 20.9, and StreamingLLM 15.5; exact string retrieval was 57.1 full versus 6.3/6.1/0.4 for PyramidKV/SnapKV/StreamingLLM. Qwen2.5-72B averaged 52.9 full versus 50.1 MInference, 39.1 KIVI, 26.5 SnapKV, and 18.9 StreamingLLM. MInference's relative success is important but it retains an O(n) KV store and sparsifies access; it is not bounded retention. Earlier [H2O](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14048) (up to 29× throughput/1.9× lower latency) and [Scissorhands](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17118) (5× KV reduction, 20× with 4-bit) did not test the required instruction-plus-agent combination. `[V]` The [ACL 2026 pitfalls study](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.1926/) tested all 541 modified IFEval prompts on Llama-3-8B and Qwen2.5-14B: StreamingLLM, H2O, K-Norm, SnapKV, and TOVA degraded instruction classes at different rates, could completely ignore some instructions, and were sensitive to instruction order. Explicit fair partitioning/whitelisting improved every policy (e.g. StreamingLLM composite score +.196 ± .043), evidence that attention did not discover semantic importance by itself.

**There is substantial adjacent prior art; G1 is not novel as “hidden state predicts retention.”** `[V]` [Fast KVzip](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17668) distills reconstruction-based KV importance into hidden-state gates in under one H100-hour and reports near-lossless 70% eviction; [Locret](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.01805) learns retaining heads from future answer attention; [DuoAttention](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10819) learns which heads require full retrieval (therefore still keeps O(n) KVs there); [Dynamic Memory Compression](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=tDRYrAkOB7) trains learned merge/write behavior with next-token loss; [AttentionPredictor (NeurIPS 2025)](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/c9ffae6711fb9c28121795c80ec77b70-Abstract-Conference.html) predicts next attention and reports 13× compression/5.6× offload speedup. KVzip, R-KV, Q-Filters, GemFilter, LazyLLM, MInference, and Finch are reconstruction-, reasoning-, geometry-, early-layer-, deferred-prefill-, sparse-access-, or *known-prompt*-guided methods, not causal future-turn selectors; Sirius is contextual-sparsity correction, and Memory Decoder is parametric retrieval/domain adaptation. `[V]` The very recent [TwinKV preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27128) is the closest G0 test: deleting each of 136 chunks and measuring gold-answer log-probability drop produced Spearman ρ=-.004 (p=.96) with received attention. Its deletion intervention is not deployment-matched KV eviction, so this is a warning, not a refutation.

**Self-notes help agents, but do not yet beat learned selectors in a controlled agentic comparison.** `[V]` [MemGym](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20833) reports rolling summary on τ²-bench: 50.0→58.7 (+8.7 points, n=288, 2.29× compression); structured memory on WebArena-Infinity: 34.3→38.6 (+4.3, n=140). The same summary was neutral for Sonnet on SWE-Gym and harmful for Haiku (-1.0) and GPT-OSS-120B (-3.2). Its learned compression critic achieved IID AUROC .985, but aggregate OOD AUROC was ~.43 on both unseen strategies and WebArena; selective AUROC .714/.748 covered only 26.5%/20.4%. This is unusually direct evidence against assuming transfer. A-Mem led summary/retrieval only on MemGym's synthetic 500K-token code-QA and 5/6-hop research tasks. [A-MEM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12110) reports ~1,200 tokens and 5.4 s (GPT-4o-mini) or 1.1 s (local Llama-3.2-1B) per memory operation; [ReadAgent](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09727) gets 3.5–20× effective context by gist plus raw-text lookup, but only document QA. [MEMENTO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.09852) gets ~2.5× peak-KV reduction/~1.75× throughput after training on 228K reasoning traces, not dialogues. `[I]` The best-supported pattern is **lossy index + lossless lookup**, not recursive summary alone; published wall-clock comparisons are too inconsistent to claim a universal latency win.

## Minimum decisive G0/G1/G2 experiment

**Data and split.** Use 400 Multi-IF sessions, all 176 BFCL non-cohort `long_context` cases, and 400 OASST2-4K branches (976 conversations). Candidate units are role-tagged 64–128-token spans from system/schema, user, assistant, and tool messages; cap at 12 stratified candidates/dialogue. Perform all three leave-one-*corpus*-out rotations, with template/tool-family grouping inside corpora. Never tune on the held-out corpus.

**G0 oracle.** At span end, remove only that span's KV columns—do not delete/re-tokenize text—and teacher-force the identical subsequent trajectory. Score
`u(s)=mean_future[NLL_evicted(gold assistant/tool-call tokens)-NLL_full]`, clipping negative values only for “utility mass,” while retaining signed deltas for harm analysis. This is deployment-matched and label-free. Literal provenance is a secondary diagnostic, never the label (it misses derivation and nonliteral constraints). Because leave-one-out utilities are non-additive, evaluate every selected *set* with one joint-eviction replay and spot-check 10% with greedy conditional ablations.

**G1 and G2.** Freeze Qwen; cache layer-20 mean/max/final-token states and fit one linear ranker (then, only if needed, a 2-layer MLP) to signed/ranked G0 utility. Compare at identical K=8192 and identical echo-token budget with: full, recent+sinks, H2O, SnapKV, TOVA, KVzip, rolling self-note, BM25/embedding query-time archive retrieval, and role/length/age-matched random spans. Protecting schemas is crossed as an ablation. Deploy as KV pin + verbatim echo; score Multi-IF rule pass, BFCL executable function-call accuracy, OASST teacher NLL, latency, and tokens.

**Pre-register the floor.** On *each* unseen corpus: AUPRC ≥2× prevalence and AUROC ≥.70; recover ≥50% of G0-vs-random utility at the fixed budget; jointly evicted G1 is no worse than the best G2 and better than matched random with a paired 95% CI excluding zero. For the two task-scored corpora, require ≥5-point improvement over random and no >2-point regression versus the best non-oracle G2. Failure on BFCL is failure to generalize, regardless of pooled average. `[I]` Probe fitting from cached features is minutes on CPU and easily “hours on one GPU”; exhaustive G0 is not safely an hours claim. At ≤11,712 branches it is plausibly an overnight one-GPU job only with prefix reuse/batched branches. KVP's 56-GPU trace collection and MemGym's 8×A100, ~3-hour 32K critic training make a one-GPU end-to-end promise unjustified.

## Miller framing and “do not do this”

`[V]` Miller, Lundqvist & Bastos's [Working Memory 2.0](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8112390/) proposes activity-silent synaptic weight changes refreshed by sparse spiking; [Lundqvist et al. 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5220584/) observed brief gamma (45–100 Hz) bursts at a minority of PFC sites encoding/reactivating remembered items, with beta (20–35 Hz) as a competing/default control state. [Lundqvist et al. 2018](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-017-02791-8) ties burst dynamics to readout, and [Miller & Buschman](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3709832/) argues synchronized oscillations route/select distributed *neural representations* for limited processing. `[I]` A static hidden-state classifier choosing text spans matches only the abstract separation “latent store/control selects.” It has no oscillation, phase, burst, synchrony, anatomical routing, or synaptic substrate; call it **Miller-inspired engineering**, not evidence for “waves select.”

**Do not:** (1) use answer literals as G0; (2) train/test by random conversation split; (3) label with attention and then claim causal need; (4) delete text to simulate KV eviction; (5) report token-level AUROC without joint-eviction and task success; (6) compare unequal echo/cache budgets; (7) keep schemas in the ordinary eviction pool without an explicit ablation; (8) treat LoCoMo/LongMemEval QA, RULER needles, OASST perplexity, or summary quality as agentic transfer; (9) permanently discard raw history; or (10) spend a full 1K-dialogue oracle run before a 30-dialogue/corpus timing and signal sanity check.
