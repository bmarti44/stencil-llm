Fit-on: none. Development-on: two new Kimi K3/Ollama conversations authored from the mechanism specification, plus synthetic CPU controls; design informed by exposed earlier maintenance and cold-diagnostic results. Evaluated-on: none. No benchmark examples, old evaluation outputs, gold labels or author rationales enter model prompts. New data is exposed DEV, not fresh validation in the statistical sense.

Question: can plain-language memory preserve the correct active obligations over a changing conversation, with each step receiving only its own actual notes and authenticated natural source history? This tests the maintenance prerequisite to the larger coding-agent goal. Automatically matching good manual prose is valuable; it need not prove that structured registers outperform prose. It does not establish long-session generalization or competent coding.

Two independently audited, original Kimi conversations, eight turns each. Coverage includes multiple rules, global/task scope, permissions versus requirements, replacement, withdrawal, restoration while replacement is live, cancellation of a task override, one-off requests, unadopted quotations, tool-source instructions and explicit adoption.
