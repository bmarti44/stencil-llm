**Park the failed recurrent-updater line. A source-only, request-time reader is a substantially different dataflow worth considering, but removing the notes alone is not an evidence-backed fix.** Its output would answer “which obligations apply to this request?” and then be discarded. Persistent memory would consist only of original authenticated messages. Nothing generated by this reader becomes input to its next invocation.

The [failed trial's protocol](PROTOCOL.md) already supplied every preceding natural message as well as the previous actual note. Consequently this proposal supplies no missing history. Its differences are eliminating the feedback edge from generated notes and asking for one applicable view instead of maintaining a complete state for all future requests. Calling another all-scopes note updater “reconstruction,” or merely adding “trust the history,” would not meet the three-failure stop-loss. The coordinating agent's final audit credits the entire JavaScript trajectory and reports eight definite global-view errors and six ambiguous FFI views for Rust: 34 unambiguous correct views overall and one of two correct trajectories. ## Evidence and its limits

LongMemEval found that replacing original sessions/rounds with extracted summaries or facts generally hurt QA through information loss; fact compression helped its multi-session reasoning subset. It also found imperfect reading even when retrieval returned all evidence. These findings support retaining originals and separating evidence availability from correct interpretation. They do not test Qwen3's scoped instruction lifecycle. Importantly, Stencil already retained those originals, so the representation result is not direct evidence that deleting its extra notes helps. [Wu et al., LongMemEval, revision 4 March 2025, sections 5.2–5.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10813v2).

Mem0's authors describe replacing reconciliation with additive extraction because overwrites/deletions sometimes destroyed useful information. Their release also changes retrieval and reports managed-platform results with proprietary optimizations; it does not isolate removal of updates. Furthermore, additive **generated facts** are not immutable **source messages**, and preserving conflicting facts does not itself resolve which instruction applies now. The authors themselves identify remaining temporal/event-ordering weaknesses. This is a relevant engineering precedent, not causal proof or a recommendation to adopt their service. [Deshraj Yadav, Mem0, 16 April 2026](https://mem0.ai/blog/mem0-the-token-efficient-memory-algorithm).

Counterevidence matters: recursive summarization has produced more consistent long-context dialogue in published experiments. Generated notes are not universally harmful. Conversely, full-context reading can depend strongly on where evidence appears, even in models designed for long contexts. Neither study establishes the behavior of this pinned Qwen3 or these short eight-turn histories. [Wang et al., Recursively Summarizing, revision 25 August 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.15022v4); [Liu et al., Lost in the Middle, revision 20 November 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03172v3).

**Mechanism hypothesis, not measured fact:** a wrong self-note can anchor later interpretations despite available source evidence. Removing it prevents that particular error from being carried forward. It cannot prevent the reader from independently making the same scope mistake on every query. If the first narrowing occurred while the incoming note was still correct, propagation of that error cannot explain its onset. A disposable answer can also differ across tasks or repeated queries. Failure stops propagating through persisted notes; semantic correctness remains unproven.

## What must remain true

These are deductions from Stencil's contract, not literature guarantees:

- Keep chronological message IDs, authentic roles/origins, original text, current request kind and requested task. Assistant/tool text can be needed to understand a later user's adoption, but never acquires instruction authority just because it is retrieved. Do not simply discard all non-user context.
- Reconstruct applicable obligations, including global rules and relevant task overrides. Do not equate “current task” with “scope of every rule mentioned nearby.” Preserve permission versus requirement and unrelated global obligations.
- Read replacements, withdrawals, restorations and override cancellation in order. “Newest relevant sentence wins” fails when the newer sentence changes a different scope or restores an older rule. Removing writable state moves this reasoning to read time; it does not remove it.
- Associate each returned obligation with source IDs and a short exact supporting span, including later modifying/adopting messages where needed. A valid substring proves location, not scope, authority, survival or completeness. Score those meanings independently. An omitted obligation has no citation to check.

Use the entire short source history first. Retrieval adds an avoidable missing-evidence failure here. If history later exceeds a measured budget, retrieve original messages with chronological context and the full relevant change chain, including withdrawals and adoption antecedents. Semantic similarity to the current coding task may miss global rules or a terse cancellation. Any derived index may suggest original records to read; it must not become the authoritative rule store. LongMemEval's fact-enriched keys preserve original values, but its QA results do not establish complete lifecycle-chain recall. [LongMemEval, section 5.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10813v2).

## Smallest discriminating test to register, not launch

Propose a **four-call mechanism diagnostic**, not another full maintenance attempt. Freeze the already-exposed Rust history at the earliest independently confirmed scope error. Choose two request scopes whose expected applicability distinguishes the global rule from the erroneous task-only interpretation. Use the same original history, request-specific output contract, source-evidence requirement and pinned model/settings in both arms:

1. Source-only reconstruction: no previous generated note anywhere in the request.
2. Identical reconstruction with the actual erroneous saved note additionally provided as a non-authoritative prior model note. Do not hand-correct or strengthen it.

Two scopes times two arms equals four calls. Each call is independent; discard its answer after saving the receipt. This tests exposure to an existing self-note under the proposed request-time contract, not natural generation of that note or the entire architectural effect. Do not compare only with the old updater and claim a causal note ablation: the old output task differs too.

Predeclare semantic scoring: all applicable obligations, correct scope and modality, no inventions or retired-rule leakage, and evidence that actually supports each interpretation. Source-only correct in both scopes and note-exposed wrong supports interference at this one snapshot. Both correct supports neither the interference claim nor a reliable fix; narrower querying or prompt differences may explain improvement over the historical updater. Both wrong means note removal did not solve the observed semantic problem. Source-only worse rejects the proposed advantage there. Any transport/cap error is incomplete evidence, not a semantic null. No retries or prompt repair within this test.

Four calls cannot validate withdrawal, restoration, authority handling or long sessions. Any subsequent feasibility proposal must predeclare complete applicable-view checks across independently authored development histories covering those mechanisms, retain the three failed recipes in the record, and obtain separate authorization. This memo authorizes neither the four calls nor that follow-up. No model switch, training or retrieval build is justified by the cited evidence alone.

## Cost and confidence

Let H be original-history tokens, N prior-note tokens, and O output tokens. The failed recipe already paid roughly H+N input tokens per update. Source-only reading at an equivalent snapshot removes N but still reads H and emits O; there is no demonstrated large token saving. It can avoid maintenance calls on non-query turns, while separate task queries can multiply read calls. Over T equally sized history increments of h tokens, one uncached full-prefix read per increment submits hT(T+1)/2 history tokens in total; this arithmetic is an input-volume estimate, not wall-time or GPU complexity. Record startup separately, total tokens, per-request latency and full workload cost before proposing an operating advantage.

vLLM documents prefix caching as reusing common-prefix prefill work, without reducing output decoding time. It may help a stable source-history layout, but does not remove context limits, semantic errors or all long-history cost. No claim is made that this facility or its current API matches the installed pinned image; measure actual reuse before crediting savings. [vLLM contributors, Automatic Prefix Caching, living documentation](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/features/automatic_prefix_caching/).
