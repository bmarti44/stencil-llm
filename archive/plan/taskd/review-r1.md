SCORE: 24

## Findings

1. **critical — Task D remains exactly latch-shaped.** The complete task state is still “the most recent one-of-eight cue.” B3 computes that sufficient statistic exactly, regardless of droughts, bursts, or sequence length. There are no accumulated instructions, targeted modifications, revocations, scopes, or interacting constraints. The schedules stress retention duration, but not richer instruction tracking. The benchmark therefore structurally precludes an oscillator-specific advantage while recreating the defect that motivated the pivot.

2. **critical — Teacher-forced answers leak the active rule.** Reusing the current evaluation path feeds every gold answer token back into later query contexts. Under the Latin-square construction, an operand/answer pair uniquely identifies the active rule for k=8. Consequently, later queries can recover the rule from a recent gold answer rather than retain the instruction. Multiple queries per span make this a recurring implicit reinsertion channel. Prior answers must be hidden or neutralized, or each query evaluated from an independently constructed prefix.

3. **critical — The reinsertion incumbent is a strawman.** The model’s effective receptive field is 252 tokens, yet refresh occurs every 512. In a cue-only interval, decisions at lags 253–511 are causally blind—roughly half the cycle. A strong prompt incumbent would reinsert at compaction boundaries, within the receptive field, or immediately before queries. Moreover, a drought cannot make a copy of the “currently active instruction” stale when no update occurs; it merely becomes inaccessible between copies. Register a query-time policy and a refresh/cost sweep, including intervals no larger than 252.

4. **critical — The adequacy gate conditions away the failures being measured.** A reinsertion run with queries outside the 252-token receptive field is unlikely to reach 99% on the proposed ID distribution, while persistent-path contenders can. Saying its benchmark numbers do not “count” then removes the incumbent by construction. Filtering adequate seeds can also turn oscillator adoption variance into flattering conditional accuracy—the exact danger illustrated by Phase 3’s extreme M1 seed variance. All initial seeds must enter the primary estimand; retries should be separately reported, and any adoption failure should block a positive verdict rather than disappear from aggregation.

5. **high — The verdict can simultaneously declare benefit and no benefit.** If M1 beats reinsertion and ties B3/B4, it satisfies condition (b), but the next sentence requires the final verdict “no substrate benefit.” Seed aggregation, whether both holdouts must pass, missing-run handling, paired comparisons, training-seed uncertainty, and minimum useful tail accuracy are also unspecified. A 2% versus 0% tail result could pass the one-point rule despite being useless. Define one mutually exclusive decision table with competence floors, all-seed robustness, and accuracy–cost comparisons against the best contender.

6. **high — The benchmark omits the oscillator variant designed for changes.** M1b’s learnable damping is the project’s registered mechanism for forgetting and overwriting, yet only conservative M1 is included in the regime where supersession matters most. B2, the non-oscillatory recurrent control, is also omitted. A negative result cannot support a conclusion about the oscillator family without M1b, and a positive M1 result cannot establish that oscillation matters without an equal-capacity non-oscillatory stateful comparator.

7. **high — The holdout protocol is neither fully specified nor sealed.** “Within ~100 tokens,” “long quiet,” query placement, insertion collisions, end-of-sequence handling, and curriculum interpolation lack exact distributions and draw order. The plan names three schedule families but later requires “all four families.” Burst also overlaps the early dense-update curriculum unless its joint structure is formally disjoint. Finally, evaluating every family before deciding on the 60k retry exposes the purported final holdouts during checkpoint selection. Use separate validation and sealed final offsets, exact family definitions, and recorded schedule/config hashes.

8. **high — The headline metrics are confounded or gameable.** Distance since the original instruction ignores periodic reinsertion and rule-revealing prior answers. “Greatest bin still ≥90%” is not a half-life when refreshes create non-monotone recovery and sparse far bins can cross 90% by noise. Pooled accuracy overweights sequences or spans with more queries. Stale-rule rate grows mechanically with the number of distinct prior rules and is conditional on making an error. Freeze bin edges and minimum counts; record distance since every information source; use sequence-weighted paired metrics, first-crossing/survival summaries, and unconditional stale-error excess over the analytic null.

9. **high — The proposed variable-query generator is incompatible with the reused training stack.** Current batching requires every sequence in a batch to have exactly the same number of answer decisions. Random update counts combined with 1–3 queries per span violate that invariant; CUDA-graph execution additionally requires fixed tensor shapes across batches. The plan needs a registered padded-decision representation with a validity mask and a non-vacuous masked loss, or a fixed-count construction. Without it, the promised reuse and runtime estimate are not credible.

10. **medium — Sliding-window loss is not a realistic compaction model.** Actual compaction usually creates summaries or retained system state that may preserve, distort, or conflict with later instructions. This benchmark simply drops causal access while B4 receives privileged permanent cue retention. It does not test summary loss, repeated compactions, old-summary/new-update conflicts, or bounded external instruction memory. Results must be scoped to synthetic local-attention persistence unless at least one explicit compaction-and-summary baseline is added.

11. **medium — Cost and deterministic evidence are under-budgeted.** Moving from 20k×2048 to 40k×4096 is roughly four times M1’s training token work before multi-family evaluation; B4 has distinct growth with accumulated cue positions. One M1 pilot cannot size B4 or the reinsertion pipeline. Pilot each materially different cost class, include evaluation time and peak memory, and define how a bitwise rerun is retained without overwriting the first run’s evidence.

## Alignment verdict

No. Executing this plan would answer the narrower question “can a persistent pathway beat a deliberately infrequent refresh policy on a one-token most-recent-rule task under schedule shift?” It would not answer whether the oscillator architecture provides real-world benefit for long-horizon instruction following as additions and changes accumulate. The latch-shaped target, answer leakage, weak incumbent, adequacy filtering, and contradictory verdict can manufacture or erase the claimed benefit. Those central elements require redesign before implementation.

## Unverified surfaces

Task D has not been implemented, so exact schedule draws, reinsertion collision semantics, curriculum behavior, sealed-split integrity, per-bin counts, retry orchestration, and 4096-token throughput cannot be verified. Python summaries of available Phase 3 artifacts reproduce M1’s approximately 44.6% k=32 mean and B3’s perfect completed cells; the current results snapshot does not independently expose the complete claimed B4 matrix, so that fact is accepted from the supplied context.
